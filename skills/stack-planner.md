# stack-planner

## read-the-need-against-the-stack

Takes in what somebody says they need, names the process that would run
inside the tool, and reads the stack for something already licensed that
comes near. A need with no process behind it goes back, because the
thing being asked for is a way of working rather than a tool.

covers:
- Take in the Need
- Name the Process It Serves
- Check the Stack for It

## fix-the-requirements-before-any-candidate-is-seen

Writes the requirements and the scoring criteria down, dates them at a
version, and puts them in front of every agent in the evaluation at
once. Nothing is judged against a demonstration, because the list
existed first.

covers:
- Write down the Requirements
- Set the Scoring Criteria
- Brief the Evaluation

## narrow-the-field-to-finalists

Works the market down to a shortlist, naming the requirement each
dropped candidate failed. Runs the finalists on the work the team really
has, over the same period, adds up what each one costs over its term,
and sets them side by side against one list.

covers:
- Search the Market
- Cut to a Shortlist
- Trial the Finalists on Real Work
- Work out the Whole Cost
- Compare the Finalists

## put-the-choice-to-a-named-person

Puts the comparison in front of a person who picks one and says why. The
same happens at a review, where the answer is keep it, change it or
retire it. The choice is never this agent's.

covers:
- Choose the Tool
- Decide Keep, Change or Retire

## hold-the-standing-review

Holds the review that asks whether a tool still earns its place, and
records the decision with its date, the evidence it was made on and the
date the next one falls. A review that did not happen is recorded as not
held rather than left blank.

covers:
- Run the Standing Review
- Record the Review

## keep-the-stack-record

Writes the chosen tool into the stack record and keeps that record true
afterwards, so every entry names the process it serves, its owner, its
cost, its term and its renewal date. A retirement closes an entry with
what replaced it rather than removing it.

covers:
- Take in the Chosen Tool
- Write It into the Stack Record
- Update the Stack Record

## move-a-process-out-of-a-tool-that-is-going

Takes a decision to retire, names where the work goes next, sees it
moved there, and files what the retirement taught. The tool stays on
until a home is named and somebody has agreed to take the work.

covers:
- Take in the Decision to Retire
- Name Where the Process Goes
- Move the Process to Its New Home
- Record What Was Learned
