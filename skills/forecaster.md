# forecaster

## read-the-record

Reads the organization's own record of its customers and says what is in it: who bought, who stayed, who left, when each one arrived, and what each one pays today.

covers:
- Read the Affected Customers
- Read the Base over Time
- Read the Customer Record

## cut-a-population-into-groups

Cuts a customer base or a market into groups that behave differently, describes each group and how buying works inside it, and checks that each group really does hold together and stand apart from the rest.

covers:
- Group the Base
- Map the Market and Its Segments
- Test Whether the Segments Hold Apart

## find-the-traits-that-separate-groups

Works out which traits separate one group from another, or separate the wins from everything else, and says which of those traits the division should be made on.

covers:
- Choose the Dividing Traits
- Find What the Groups Share
- Find the Traits that Predict a Win

## work-out-which-signals-are-usable

Assembles the facts that might predict a fit or a win, records where each one comes from, and measures how often each is actually filled in and what it costs to get.

covers:
- Gather the Candidate Signals
- Name the Qualifying Signals
- Test the Signal Coverage

## draft-two-or-three-candidates

Writes two or three candidate answers to the question on the table, each one written down with the evidence and the assumptions under it.

covers:
- Draft the Candidate Mixes
- Draft the Candidate Models
- Draft the Candidate Profiles
- Draft the Strategic Options
- Draw the Candidate Divisions
- Fit the Candidate Models

## run-a-candidate-model-over-the-record

Runs a candidate model or profile over what already happened and reports how well it would have called it, and runs it beside what is in force today to show what would come out differently.

covers:
- Back-Test the Candidates
- Check What Each Does to Routing
- Read How the Model Has Scored
- Recompute the Closed Periods
- Run It Beside the Live Model
- Test the Candidates against the Record
- Test the Persona against the Record

## size-a-market

Says how many buyers or accounts of a given shape exist and how much money sits with them. It picks the counting method and says why, sets the price and frequency assumptions with the source of each one, runs the count through each method, and explains where the methods disagree.

covers:
- Choose the Sizing Method
- Compute the Size Each Way
- Count the Buyers
- Reconcile the Methods
- Set the Rate Assumptions
- Size Each Candidate Market
- Size Each Segment

## put-cost-and-return-on-an-option

Works out what an option would cost and what it would return at each level of spend, with every assumption written down beside the number it produced. A rate the agent cannot source is reported as unsourced rather than filled in with a figure that looks reasonable.

covers:
- Choose the Route
- Cost Each Way of Closing the Gap
- Model Each Mix
- Price the Incentives
- Price the Requests
- Put Numbers on Each Initiative
- Put Numbers on Each Option
- Put Numbers on Each Request
- Put Numbers on the Choices
- Put Numbers on the Proposed Changes
- Size What Each Move Returns
- Work out the Whole Cost

## size-the-work-and-the-shortfall

Works out how much work something takes and whether there is enough to carry it, which covers the hours one run costs, the load a new tool would take on, and where the demand passes what the department can do.

covers:
- Find the Shortfall
- Size the Effort Each Run Takes
- Size What the Tool Has to Carry

## set-the-money-to-hold-back

Says how much money to set aside and for how long, whether that is what an engagement may cost over its term or what is held back for changes during the year.

covers:
- Set the Budget and the Term
- Size the Reserve

## test-the-assumptions

Moves each assumption one at a time and records how far the answer swings, so it is clear which assumptions the number actually depends on.

covers:
- Test the Assumptions

## project-against-the-goal

Projects forward from where things stand now and says what the rest of the period looks like and whether what is being asked for can reach the goals already set.

covers:
- Re-Forecast the Rest of the Year
- Test the Asks against the Goals

## rank-and-score-against-criteria

Scores each candidate against criteria that were fixed beforehand and puts them in order, naming the criterion behind every verdict and the evidence each place rests on.

covers:
- Rank the Segments
- Score the Proposals

## record-what-a-number-rests-on

Writes down what a number rests on, which means the assumptions, the boundary, the method, the window it covers, and what would reopen it.

covers:
- Record What the Budget Rests On
- Record What the Plan Rests On
- Record What the Review Rests On
- Record What the Round Rests On
- Write down the Sizing

## put-the-work-in-front-of-the-people-who-act

Puts the finished work in front of the people who decide and the agents that act on it, each option carrying its cost and what it rules out, and takes their reading of it before it issues.

covers:
- Put the Options to the People Who Decide
- Review the Findings
