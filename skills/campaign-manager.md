# campaign-manager

## run-intake

Reads the request that starts a run and writes down what it has to
achieve, who it is for, what it may spend and when it is due. Everything
the run does afterwards is checked back against what is recorded here.

covers:
- Take in the Account List
- Take in the Audience
- Take in the Audience and the Goal
- Take in the Brief
- Take in the Budget and Goal
- Take in the Creative and the Plan
- Take in the Lapsed Segment
- Take in the Partner Agreement
- Take in the Question
- Take in the Request
- Take in the Targeting Need

## maker-briefs

Puts the same request in front of everyone who has to make something for
the run at the same time, saying what is needed, what has to be said and
by when, so nobody works from an older version of it.

covers:
- Brief the Creatives
- Brief the Creators
- Brief the Department
- Brief the Makers

## request-collection

Asks every area and every agent what it wants run or made, in one format
and against one due date, and brings what comes back together as one
list to work from.

covers:
- Ask Each Area What It Would Change
- Collect What Each Function Asks For
- Collect What the Plan Must Carry
- Collect the Candidate Initiatives
- Collect the Topic Candidates

## commitments-and-capacity

Reads what the period already owes and what is still free, which means
the work already in flight with its fixed dates, the processes a plan
breaks down into and how often each runs, and how much each agent can
take on.

covers:
- Break the Work into Activities
- List What Is Already Committed
- List the Committed Work
- Work out What Is Free

## calendar-and-flighting

Lays the work out across the period with dates on it, says when each
channel runs and at what weight, and settles what stops or waits when
something new takes a place that was already taken.

covers:
- Build the Calendar
- Lay out the Flighting
- Settle What Gets Displaced

## campaign-direction

Weighs the options until one is worth backing and records what the run
will lead with, whether that is the concept behind a campaign, the
concept two organizations will both put their name on, or the offer that
goes in front of a customer.

covers:
- Settle on a Direction
- Settle the Joint Direction
- Settle the Offer

## run-parameters

Settles the practical terms the run works to, such as the placements,
the dates, the money on it and what a result is worth, and where two
organizations run together, the share each side pays and what each owes
the other.

covers:
- Plan the Joint Campaign
- Set the Parameters

## audience-treatment

Divides the audience into groups and says how much each group gets, from
the money and attention a tier of accounts is worth down to who gets a
message, who gets a call and who is left alone, and names who is kept
out and which groups must not overlap.

covers:
- Name the Exclusions
- Set the Tiers and the Weight
- Sort the Segment into Treatments

## contact-coordination

Settles who approaches which account and in what order, and spaces the
approaches out so that no account is contacted twice in the same period.

covers:
- Agree the Split with Sales
- Keep the Touches in Step

## test-design

Names the one element a test changes and what it should improve, sets
how much exposure it needs and how big a difference has to be before it
counts, and says what that exposure will cost and whether to pay it.

covers:
- Commit the Test Budget
- Name the Change to Test
- Set the Evidence Bar

## test-decision

Reads a finished test and calls a winner, no difference, or more time,
then puts the winner in place of what was running and takes the losing
versions off.

covers:
- Call the Winner
- Roll out the Winner

## go-live

Starts the run on the date that was agreed, once the person who owns it
says go, and confirms that what should be running is running, whether
that is a set of campaigns at their opening bids, the ads, the posts,
the messages or the variants of a test at their split.

covers:
- Build the Campaigns and Launch
- Launch
- Launch the Campaigns
- Launch the Joint Campaign
- Launch the Test
- Send the Sequence
- Start the Touches
- Switch the Ads On

## promotion

Runs the email, social and paid work that fills a session, an event or a
place, pointed at the action the run wants somebody to take, and answers
what comes back.

covers:
- Promote the Conference
- Promote the Event
- Promote the Piece
- Promote the Presence
- Promote the Session

## owned-channel-publication

Puts finished material out on the channels the organization and its
creators control, on the dates that were agreed, and answers the replies
it brings in.

covers:
- Publish It on the Owned Channels
- Publish the Material
- Publish the Posts

## content-reuse

Takes what a piece of work already produced and turns it into further
pieces, such as clips, posts, written summaries and a page that holds
them together.

covers:
- Repurpose the Content

## in-flight-adjustment

Reads what is running against what it was meant to do, says which lines
should give money up and which should take it, moves the money, and
swaps out creative that has stopped working.

covers:
- Name the Candidate Moves
- Optimize

## performance-watch

Tracks a run while it is live and sets what is coming back against what
the brief promised, including the responses that ought to worry
somebody, such as complaints and opt-outs.

covers:
- Watch Performance
- Watch the Joint Campaign
- Watch the Response

## campaign-report

Reports what a run did against what it was asked to do, with the cost of
each result, and puts the report in front of the agents it names so they
can confirm the figures before it issues.

covers:
- Account for the Campaigns
- Check the Account
- Report Engagement
- Report against the Goal
- Report the Win-Back

## close-out

Closes a run down at the end, which means taking the advertising off,
pulling the final numbers, and closing out the contacts that never
answered.

covers:
- Retire the Ones Who Stayed Silent
- Wrap Up

## handover

Gives the settled thing to every agent and function that has to work
from it, and takes back from each one what it is going to change as a
result.

covers:
- Hand It to the Campaign
- Hand over the Approved Topics
- Hand over the Commitments
- Hand over the Map
- Hand over the Model
- Hand over the Persona
- Hand over the Profile
- Hand over the Segments
- Hand over the Selling Kit
- Hand the Plan to the Buying Agents
- Hand the Strategy to the Work that Follows
- Tell Every Function What It Has
- Tell the Agents that Read the Plan

## live-state-record

Writes down at a version what is actually in force right now, such as
which creative sits on which line and the pools, caps and exclusions the
run is operating under, so a later reading can be set against it.

covers:
- Record What Is Live
- Record What Is in Force

## campaign-setup

Stands up the practical pieces a run needs before it starts, such as the
page and form a reader lands on, the advertising accounts, the tracking
and the schedule.

covers:
- Build the Registration Page
- Set up the Practical Side

## direct-outreach

Puts the approach in front of named parties one at a time, such as a
partner the program wants to recruit or an account where the owner makes
the contact in person.

covers:
- Call the Named Accounts
- Open Recruitment

## learning-record

Files what the run taught, which means what to repeat and what to avoid
the next time the same work comes round.

covers:
- Record What Was Learned
