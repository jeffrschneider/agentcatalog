# decision-coordinator

## take-in-the-question

Takes in whatever opened the decision, whether that is a change, a target, a gap, a request or a scheduled cycle, and works out what the decision has to settle and by when.

covers:
- Take in the Approved Positioning
- Take in the Change
- Take in the Company Targets
- Take in the Cycle
- Take in the Drift or the Signal
- Take in the Gap
- Take in the Naming Request
- Take in the Objectives
- Take in the Period's Goals
- Take in the Persona
- Take in the Planning Guidance
- Take in the Planning Trigger
- Take in the Question
- Work out What Has to Be Decided

## read-what-is-already-settled

Reads what is already in force before a new decision opens, which means the standards at the versions they are held at, and the questions and commitments the last meeting left standing.

covers:
- Read the Last Meeting's Record
- Read the Standards in Force

## set-the-boundary-of-the-question

Says what is inside the question and what sits outside it, which covers where the market ends, what the round or the review takes in, the date it has to decide by, and what a buyer would pick instead.

covers:
- Draw the Market Boundary
- Name the Competing Choices
- Open the Round
- Set the Market Boundary
- Set What the Review Covers

## settle-the-criteria-before-the-work-runs

Fixes the rules the work will be held to before any candidate arrives, so that nothing is scored against a standard invented after the fact.

covers:
- Choose the Dividing Traits
- Choose the Sizing Method
- Name the Qualifying Signals
- Set the Naming Criteria
- Set the Selection Criteria

## name-what-it-will-be-judged-by

Names the numbers the answer will be reported against and says where each one is read from.

covers:
- Name What Will Be Measured
- Name What the Strategy Will Be Judged By
- Set the Goals for the Year
- Settle What the Wave Measures

## collect-what-every-area-puts-forward

Sends one request in one format with one due date to every area or agent, and gathers what comes back into a single list.

covers:
- Ask Each Area What It Would Change
- Collect What Each Function Asks For
- Collect the Candidate Initiatives
- Collect the Topic Candidates

## name-what-is-missing

Sets what exists against what the standard or the plan asks for, and names what is missing, which of the misses matter, and which sit inside the noise.

covers:
- Audit What the Company Says Now
- Find the Gaps
- Name the Gaps Worth Acting On
- Name the Gaps that Remain

## draft-the-candidate-answers

Commissions the evidence and writes up two or three candidate answers to the open question, each one carrying the case for it.

covers:
- Draft the Candidate Positions
- Draft the Candidate Profiles
- Draft the Stages
- Draft the Strategic Options
- Draw the Candidate Divisions
- Generate the Candidate Names

## narrow-the-candidates-to-a-shortlist

Takes candidates off the list when they break the criteria, and leaves a short list worth spending time and money on.

covers:
- Cut to a Shortlist
- Screen the Long List

## score-and-rank-the-candidates

Scores each candidate against the criteria already fixed and puts them in one order, with the reason for every place and the evidence it rests on.

covers:
- Compare the Finalists
- Rank the Requests
- Rank the Segments
- Score the Candidates
- Weigh the Requests against the Goals

## check-the-evidence-holds-up

Reads what is proposed against the standards and the goals already in force, and where two sets of numbers disagree it sets out the disagreement and what explains it.

covers:
- Check the Options against What Is Agreed
- Reconcile the Methods
- Test the Asks against the Goals

## fit-the-work-to-the-capacity

Works out what an option would need in money and in people, what the period already owes and how much can be carried, then lays the committed work across the period with an agent, a date and a budget on each item and says what in flight has to stop or wait.

covers:
- Build the Calendar
- Check What Each Option Would Take
- Check What the Department Can Carry
- List the Committed Work
- Resource the Commitments
- Settle What Gets Displaced

## prepare-the-decision-pack

Settles what will be asked of the room and what the department will say, and gets the pack to everyone with time to read it before they walk in.

covers:
- Send the Pack Ahead
- Settle Marketing's Position

## convene-the-room-where-it-is-decided

Convenes the people who decide, puts each option in front of them with its cost and what it rules out, runs the session, and afterwards goes and gets answers to the questions the room could not settle.

covers:
- Answer What Was Left Open
- Deliver the Briefing
- Hold the Review
- Put the Options to the People Who Decide

## record-the-choice-that-was-made

Takes the answer the named deciders picked and writes it down with the reasons they gave and the names of anyone who disagreed. This agent picks none of it itself, which is what makes the record worth trusting later.

covers:
- Agree Who the Company Is For
- Agree the Calendar
- Choose What Gets Committed
- Choose Where to Play and How to Win
- Choose the Agency
- Choose the Identity
- Choose the Initiatives
- Choose the Mix
- Choose the Model
- Choose the Name
- Choose the Position
- Choose the Route
- Choose the Tool
- Decide What Happens to Each Surface
- Decide What Happens to Published Reports
- Decide What Is Handed Over
- Decide the Mix
- Reset the Commitments
- Set the Switch Date and the Order
- Settle What May Be Said
- Settle on One Division
- Settle on One Profile
- Settle on the Number
- Settle the Map
- Settle the Tiers and the Terms

## settle-how-the-money-is-split

Records how the money is divided and moved, which covers the split across functions and initiatives, when each part becomes available, how much is held back, who may move money and when, and each move the person who owns the money agrees to.

covers:
- Allocate the Budget
- Decide the Move
- Move the Money
- Phase the Money across the Year
- Set the Rules for Moving Money
- Settle the Split across Functions
- Size the Reserve

## write-down-what-was-agreed

Writes the settled answer down in full, holding what was decided, what was turned down and why, and who disagreed with it.

covers:
- Record the Decisions
- Record the Declines
- Write down the Agreed Map
- Write down the Agreed Profile
- Write down the Agreed Segments
- Write down the Agreed Strategy
- Write down the Sizing

## record-what-the-answer-rests-on

Writes down what the answer rests on and what would reopen it, including the assumptions, the figures behind a change, and the date the answer gets looked at again.

covers:
- Record What the Budget Rests On
- Record What the Plan Rests On
- Record What the Review Rests On
- Record What the Round Rests On
- Record Why the Money Moved
- Set the Review Conditions

## write-the-words-the-company-will-use

Writes the statement the organization stands behind, the pillars and proof points under it, and the version each audience gets.

covers:
- Build the Messaging Architecture
- Write the Messaging
- Write the Positioning and the Promise

## issue-the-answer-at-a-version

Issues the agreed answer at a numbered version on the date it is due, and tells every reader that the version is out.

covers:
- Issue the Positioning at a Version
- Publish the Plan
- Publish the Reset Commitments

## hand-the-answer-to-the-agents-that-act

Gives every agent and function that has to act the same account of what was decided, and takes back from each one what it is going to change. Whoever asked for something hears the answer, including the ones that were turned down.

covers:
- Brief the Department
- Brief the Makers
- Deliver the Findings
- Deliver the Themes and Recommendations
- Hand over the Commitments
- Hand over the Map
- Hand over the Model
- Hand over the Profile
- Hand over the Segments
- Hand the Decisions On
- Hand the Strategy to the Work that Follows
- Tell Every Function What It Has
- Tell the Processes that Read It
- Tell the Requesters

## write-the-readout

Writes the readout for whoever asked for the work, with the evidence under every line and what each finding asks somebody to change.

covers:
- Report Awareness, Perception and Preference
- Write the Findings

## run-a-submission-through-sign-off

Records what was submitted and at which version, checks that the material, its claims and its sources are all there, names which standards apply and who has to sign, and gets those signatures against that one version.

covers:
- Check the Submission Is Complete
- Get the Signatures
- Name the Standards and the Signers
- Take in the Submission

## return-the-verdict-on-a-submission

Pulls every finding into one list with the rule each one broke, sends the failures back to whoever submitted them, records in writing any failure that is knowingly accepted and the reasons for it, and releases the cleared version to the requester.

covers:
- Gather the Findings into One List
- Record an Accepted Exception
- Release the Cleared Version
- Return the Failures to the Submitter

## record-what-was-learned

Writes down what is worth repeating next time and what to avoid, so the next run starts from it.

covers:
- Record What Was Learned
