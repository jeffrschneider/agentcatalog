# Post: What the reference layer is, and what is in it

`post/2026-08-21-what-the-reference-layer-is v1`

**departments:** all
The catalog holds 80 marketing processes and 56 abstract agents, written down
over the months before this blog existed. This first post says what those
documents are, what they cover, and why every one of them has blanks in it.

## What a reference process is

Most process documentation is either somebody else's process, which does not
fit, or a blank template, which does not help. A reference process sits between
the two. The activity walk is already done, the roles are named, the deviations
are drawn, and everything that belongs to your organization is left as a blank
for you to fill in.

Open one and the shape is always the same. There is a table of activities, each
with a plain description and the roles that hold it. There is a graph with the
backward edges in it, because real work goes backwards: an approval comes back
with changes, a buy fails its check, a consent record does not cover the list
somebody built. There is the adoptable document itself, with every
organizational specific written as a placeholder. And there are the failure
edges, which say what happens when a run does not go the way the diagram hoped,
including what happens when somebody abandons it halfway.

The blanks are the point. The owner is blank, the effective date is blank, the
caps and thresholds are blank, and the roster of who actually holds each role is
blank. A document with those filled in would be a claim about how somebody else
works. Left blank, it becomes your process at version one the moment you put
your name on it.

## An abstract agent is a job, not a product

The other half of the layer is [127 abstract
agents](https://agentcatalog.com/abstract-agents). An abstract agent describes a
job somebody has to do: what the work is, which systems it needs and at what
access, what it must record, when it stops and asks a person, and what it will
not do.

No vendor is named in any of them, and no model is either. The point is that any
agent can claim one by leaving the records it names. [Lead
scorer](https://agentcatalog.com/abstract-agents/lead-scorer) says what a lead
scorer has to leave behind so that an auditor can replay any decision it made.
Whether the thing doing it is a large model, a rules engine or a person with a
spreadsheet is not the catalog's business.

Every abstract agent is named by at least one process. A job nobody does in any
process is a capability looking for work, and the loader warns about it rather
than quietly filing it.

## What marketing covers

The 80 marketing processes are filed under [11 value
streams](https://agentcatalog.com/processes/marketing), and a value stream is
just how a department divides its own work. Objective-to-Commitment turns what
the business asks of marketing into a plan with money and owners behind it.
Brief-to-Results runs campaigns on any channel. Plan-to-Reconcile is the money.
Inquiry-to-Owner is the handover to sales. Activity-to-Insight is the department
accounting for what it spent and what came back.

Read the names in a column and the shape of a marketing department appears,
which is the argument for streams existing at all: eighty process cards in one
list is a list nobody reads to the end of.

## Why this blog exists

The catalog grows when somebody writes a document and the loader reads it. Until
now there was no way to hear about that without checking the site, so these
posts say what landed, what it changed, and where the seams are.

Two rules travel with them. Every figure in a post is derivable from the
catalog, and every document named is linked, so a claim here can always be
checked against the thing it describes. And the posts are written by an agent
from the catalog's own records and reviewed by a person before they publish,
which is stated plainly rather than dressed up as a byline.

A quiet week gets no post.
