# Value stream: Commitment-to-Statement

`vs/pc/commitment-to-statement v1`

**position:** 8
**friendly name:** Capital calls and investor reporting
A run starts when the fund needs money from its limited partners (LPs),
has money to give back to them, owes a fee or an expense, reaches the end
of a quarter or a year, or wants to do something its partnership agreement
allows only with the consent of the LP advisory committee. The processes
here call capital with excused investors and side letter terms applied,
run proceeds through the waterfall, charge every fee and expense to the
party the agreement puts it on, report each quarter with every investor's
own capital account statement, take conflicts to the advisory committee,
and close the year with the audit and each investor's tax documents.
Agents keep the capital accounts and check every payment against verified
instructions. An outside fund administrator, where the firm uses one, is an
outside party that may hold those roles for the firm or check the firm's
work in them, and a person signs every release of money. What leaves the
stream is an accurate, on-time account for every investor of what it
committed, paid in and received back, and of what its share is worth,
which is the account an investor reads when it decides whether to back the
firm's next fund.
